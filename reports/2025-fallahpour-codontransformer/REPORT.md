### Our recomputed DTW did not match the authors' per-protein values, and why

At L2 the Jaccard and the similarity match exactly: 260 of 260 per-protein values agree to 1e-6, maximum difference 0.000e+00 for both. The normalized DTW does not: **0 of 260 agree to 1e-6**, maximum difference 2.033e-03, mean 3.626e-04, median 2.840e-04 (`runs/logs/analyze_sourcedata.log`, `runs/logs/l2_dtw_diff_stats.log`). That is a straight miss of the pre-registered 95% clause.

The cause is in the deposited file, and the authors' own code names it. `runs/out/authors_notebook_code.txt:1736` is `minmax = minmax |> round(digits = 2)`, inside the chunk that writes `benchmark_minmax_profiles.tsv`, the profile sheet we read. The chunk that builds `benchmark_DTW_results` (line 1745) takes the unrounded in-memory profiles instead. So their per-protein DTW came from full precision and ours cannot: rounding happens at deposit only. That one line settles the question, and it was sitting in our own extracted file before the freeze; the first draft of this report argued the same point by counting decimal places in the workbook and never cited it.

The grid is firmer than "one or two decimals" suggests. All 523,068 deposited `%MinMax` values sit exactly on the 0.01 grid, maximum deviation 7.1e-15; the decimal-count histogram is 38,716 integers, 44,504 at one decimal and 439,848 at two, so the `[1, 2]` in `runs/logs/dtw_rounding_check.log:1` is stripped trailing zeros and nothing else. **Rounded to two decimals** is the right statement.

The quantisation probe in that log bounds the size of the effect and says nothing about its direction. Perturbing the published profiles by +/-0.005, the quantisation half-step, over 780 trials gives a mean |shift| of 3.51e-04, a p95 of 8.58e-04 and a maximum of 2.04e-03, which is the order of magnitude we observe. It cannot be more than that: the differences we see are one-sided. From `runs/out/l1_l2_per_protein.csv`, **211 of 260 of our values are below the authors'**, mean signed difference -2.809e-04 against mean absolute 3.626e-04, so three quarters of the residual is bias, and symmetric zero-mean noise predicts no bias at all. Comparing the maximum of 780 random draws with the maximum of 260 observations is not a test either, and the claim that the two match "to the digit on the maximum" is gone from this draft.

The direction is measurable without the unrounded profiles, and the adversarial review measured it: take the 0.01-grid profiles as truth, coarsen them one step further to 0.1 and recompute. Over 40 pairs the normalized DTW moves **down in 39 of 40**, mean signed -8.60e-03. Scaled by the ten-times-finer real step that is about -8.6e-04 against the -2.81e-04 we observe: same sign, same order. Rounding both profiles onto a grid collapses small local differences and shortens the warped path, which is why our values come in low.

The agreement also improves as fast as that explanation predicts (recomputed from `runs/out/l1_l2_per_protein.csv`): 46 of 260 proteins agree within 1e-4, 194 within 5e-4, 250 within 1e-3, 259 within 2e-3 and all 260 within 3e-3.

**One log disagrees with this section, and it is the log that is wrong.** `runs/logs/dtw_rounding_check.log:3` says "mean ~5e-4", where `runs/logs/l2_dtw_diff_stats.log:1` and a recomputation from the per-protein CSV both give 3.626e-04. That line was never computed: `scripts/dtw_rounding_check.py` printed it as a literal string in the middle of a measurement. It has been fixed to read the L2 output and print the computed mean, maximum, signed mean and the 211/260 count; the log in this folder is from the run that happened and still carries the bad line. Worth noting that the error ran in our favour — 5e-4 against a simulated 3.51e-04 would have made the agreement look about 40% worse than 3.6e-04 does.

Two further checks say the definition itself is right. Our DTW mean and sd, 0.045890 +/- 0.033764, sit within 0.0003 of the authors' 0.046171 +/- 0.033760 and round to the same 0.05 +/- 0.03. And the published %MinMax profile length is exactly the codon count minus 17 for all 260 proteins and both checkpoints, which is the 18-codon sliding window the Methods describe — recomputed from `runs/out/l1_l2_per_protein.csv`, 0 violations; no log prints it, and an earlier draft cited one that does not. The base and fine-tuned codon counts are equal for all 260 proteins, so no pair fell into the authors' `different length` branch (`runs/logs/analyze_sourcedata.log`: 0 pairs of unequal length).

### The DTW sheet is broken

Alternative reading A3 was pre-registered as a suspicion from reading the sheet header. The probe confirms it, and it is worse than suspected (`runs/logs/probe_dtw_sheet.log`).

For each of the 260 proteins, the `DTW` sheet of the Fig. 4 workbook carries **one single `normalized_distance` value repeated across every model pair**. Formally: the value depends only on `model1` for 260/260 proteins, only on `model2` for 260/260, and the matrix is symmetric for 260/260. A model compared with itself should give 0; in the deposited sheet **all 1352 diagonal entries are non-zero**, ranging from 0.0126115 to 0.24322 (`runs/logs/analyze_sourcedata.log`).

The raw `distance` column says the same thing and says it more precisely: it too is a single value per protein, 260 of 260, and `distance / (n + m)` is the deposited normalized value to the last digit. For *A. thaliana* / Adalimumab heavy chain, 41.281353997167265 / 872 = 0.047341002290329434, which is the cell. So each protein's constant is one genuine DTW output, correctly normalized, broadcast across the labels. That rules out a mangled export or a recycled float. `scripts/probe_dtw_sheet.py` read the `distance` column into the matrix and never used it; the check cost nothing and the first draft of this report missed it.

The printed matrix for *A. thaliana* / Adalimumab heavy chain is 0.047341 in all 25 cells. Recomputed from the published profiles it is 0 on the diagonal, 0.046789 for Base vs Fine-tuned, 0.196319 for Base vs IDT, 0.431686 for Genewiz vs IDT, and so on.

**Whose distance is the constant? It is the Base vs Fine-tuned one.** The probe asked this the wrong way: it matched each *row's* value to the nearest recomputed partner of that row's `model1`, over all 1352 rows, but every row of a protein carries the same number, so the near-tie it returned (Fine-tuned 407, Base 406, Twist 247, IDT 220, Genewiz 50, ICOR 22) only reflects which model's own profile sits nearest. Asked per protein instead — for each protein, which of the recomputed pairs is closest to its deposited constant? — the answer is **Base vs Fine-tuned in 106 of the 110 proteins whose profiles are short enough to recompute pairwise in pure Python** (under 260 points; the other four go to Fine-tuned/ICOR twice, Base/IDT once and Fine-tuned/Genewiz once). Median gap 3.5e-04, maximum 2.03e-03, which is the rounding residual of the section above and not the gap a coincidence would leave. The adversarial review ran the same test over a smaller subset and got 43 of 45 in the same direction.

So the deposited constant **is** the Base vs Fine-tuned distance, mislabelled as every pair. That reverses what an earlier draft concluded: **level 1 is a real check of the C3 mean.** It averages the quantity C3 names, 260 times over, from numbers the authors computed at full precision — which is also what `claims.json` says, that C3 rests on L1 and L2. What L1 cannot check is the *comparative* half of the published sentence, "minimal DTW distance between the base and fine-tuned model", because the other pairs are not in the file at all. Our recomputation can: the Base vs Fine-tuned cell is the smallest off-diagonal entry in both example matrices printed in the probe log.

**The cause is unknown, and the mechanism an earlier draft named cannot be it.** That draft blamed the chunk at line 1745 for crossing the pair labels while the profile columns stayed put. Read literally, that mechanism gives `minmax1 == minmax2` in every row and therefore a matrix of zeros, not a non-zero constant; and the nesting `comparison1 = c(model1, minmax1)` before `complete(comparison1, comparison2)` exists precisely so each profile travels with its label, which the authors' comment on the identical natural-gene chunk confirms ("# link model and minmax profiles"). The observed symptom is neither what that mechanism predicts nor what correct code predicts.

What is checkable about that chunk is stronger than a guess: **it cannot run as written.** It ends with two contradictory `select()` calls,

```
select(organism, protein, model1, model2, distance, normalized_distance) |>
select(organism, description, model1, model2, dtw_object)
```

where the second asks for `description` and `dtw_object`, which the first just dropped, and drops `protein` in favour of a `description` column this frame does not have. The plotting chunk downstream then uses `normalized_distance`, which the second `select()` also drops. So the deposited notebook's DTW chunks are mutually inconsistent and are not the code that produced the deposited sheet. Pairing lost, number real, cause unknown.

"We are reading the layout wrong" does not survive: the sheet is long-format with explicit `model1`/`model2` columns that the probe reads correctly, all 1352 diagonal entries are non-zero, which no layout convention makes sensible, and the per-protein constant is a real, correctly normalized DTW of exactly the pair C3 names.

**The figure in the paper is fine.** The paper reports four different per-pair DTW means — 0.05 +/- 0.03 for base vs fine-tuned, 0.07 +/- 0.02 for Twist and IDT, 0.24 +/- 0.11 for Genewiz and Twist, 0.40 +/- 0.15 for Genewiz and IDT (`paper/fulltext_PMC11968976.txt`). An object with one value per protein across all pairs cannot produce those four numbers. The breakage is in the deposit, downstream of the figure.

### The %MinMax table validation, and why C3 never reaches L3

%MinMax needs a codon-frequency table per organism. The authors computed it in R with `kodonz::pminmax(z = 18, y = 0, spp = species)` and the release does not say which table that pulled. The pre-registration therefore made C3 at L3 conditional: we would estimate the table, feed the repository's own `get_min_max_percentage`, and accept it only if it reproduced the authors' published profiles for the authors' published sequences to a mean absolute difference of 0.02 or better on the 0-1 scale.

Both candidates were tested on all 520 published sequences (`runs/logs/validate_minmax.log`):

| table | n | mean abs diff | median | p95 | max | verdict at the 0.02 ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P, frequencies counted from the natural DNA of the Fig. 3 Source Data, per organism | 520 | 0.1525 | 0.1252 | 0.3204 | 0.4381 | REJECT |
| K, the Kazusa tables via `python_codon_tables`, the loader the repository itself ships | 520 | 0.0378 | 0.0352 | 0.0624 | 0.0857 | REJECT |

Table P, the pre-registered candidate, is off by an order of magnitude more than the ceiling; its worst case is yeast Cas9 at 0.4381 over a 1352-point profile. Table K, added as an extra, gets within a factor of two of the ceiling but still fails everywhere: median 0.0352, worst 0.0857 on a 191-point profile. Neither is the table the authors used.

So, exactly as the pre-registration says, **C3 at L3 is reported as not checkable** and C3 rests on L1 and L2, both of which run on the authors' deposited profiles rather than on any table we built. One consequence worth stating: with the table missing, the `%MinMax` step itself is unchecked in both directions, so C3 at L2 tests our DTW on their profiles and not their profiles. This is not a negative result about the model; it is a missing input in the release. Fifty natural genes per organism were available for table P (`runs/logs/validate_minmax.log`), so the estimate was not starved of data; the table the authors used is simply a different one.

Issue #28 of the repository reports a masking bug in `pretrain.py`. `claims.json` lists it under `not_checked` with the reason: we check the released checkpoints, not the method as described. No log in this folder tests it, and nothing here could: the claim is about how the base checkpoint was produced, and testing it means pretraining from scratch, which was never in scope for a CPU-only run inside a 4-hour budget. ### The verdict against the pre-registered rule

The pre-registered rule reads: *Reproduced* needs all three claims inside their band at L1 and L2 and C1 and C2 inside their band at L3; *Not reproduced* follows from any claim outside its band at L1 or L2.

Claim by claim, against the logs:

- **C1**, Jaccard. Inside at L1 (0.737856, rounds to 0.74), inside at L2 (identical to the authors' value for all 260 proteins, max diff 0.000e+00), inside at L3 (0.7379, delta -0.0021 against +/-0.03). Clean.
- **C2**, similarity. Inside at L1 (69.262883), inside at L2 (max diff 0.000e+00, 260/260), inside at L3 (69.2629, delta +0.0029 against +/-2.0). Clean.
- **C3**, DTW. Inside at L1 on the mean (0.046171, sd 0.033760, both within 0.005 of 0.05 and 0.03), and L1 is a real check of the pair: the deposited constant is the Base vs Fine-tuned distance, computed at full precision, mislabelled as every pair. Inside at L2 on the mean (0.045890 +/- 0.033764), with little room — 0.00089 left of a 0.005 band on the mean, 18% of it, and 0.00124 on the sd. **Outside the L2 band on the second clause**: the band asks for 95% of per-protein values within 1e-6 and the logs give 0 of 260. Not checkable at L3: both %MinMax tables were rejected.

So the rule gives **Not reproduced**, and that is the label on this report. There is no third door: *Partially reproduced* also requires all three claims inside their band at L1 and L2, so it is unavailable as well.

The clause that fails is one we wrote badly. It asks for 1e-6 agreement against profiles the authors rounded to two decimals before depositing them — `round(digits = 2)` in their own notebook, extracted into `runs/out/authors_notebook_code.txt` before our freeze, where anyone on this side could have read it. Their published per-protein values came from the unrounded profiles, so nobody can rebuild them to 1e-6 from the released file, the authors included. The clause measured the precision of the deposit, not the agreement of the metric.

That is an argument for a different band next time and not for a different label now. A threshold reclassified as unmeetable after it fails never constrained anything, and this pipeline runs on the premise that the pre-registration binds. The pilot's failure on LigandMPNN was choosing the definition after seeing the number; waiving the threshold after seeing the number is the same move from the other side.

Said plainly, in the same place: C1 and C2 reproduce at every level that exists, from the deposit and from two released checkpoints that regenerate 520 sequences byte for byte. C3 reproduces on the mean and the sd at both levels where it is checkable, to within 0.0003 of the authors' values, and cannot be checked per pair, because the deposited sheet lost its pair labels, or per item, because the profiles were rounded. Ordinary language calls that partial. The frozen rule cannot say it, so the label stands and the band gets fixed before the next paper: set per-item tolerances from the precision of the deposit you are going to read — here 3e-3, which all 260 meet — and do not weld a per-item exactness clause onto a mean band with no partial-credit path.

## Notes for the authors

Thanks for depositing the generated sequences, the %MinMax profiles and the R notebook alongside the paper. With the two checkpoints public and ungated, the Fig. 4 benchmark can be regenerated on a laptop CPU in twelve minutes, and it comes back byte-identical. That is a stronger kind of reproducibility than most papers offer, and it is worth saying first. A few things we ran into:

1. **The `DTW` sheet of the Fig. 4 workbook looks like it lost its pair labels.** For each of the 260 proteins, every model pair carries the same `normalized_distance` and the same raw `distance`, and all 1352 diagonal entries (a model against itself) are non-zero, from 0.0126 to 0.2432, where they should be 0. The constant looks like one real distance broadcast over the labels: `distance / (n + m)` gives the deposited normalized value to the last digit, and per protein it is nearest to the Base vs Fine-tuned pair in 106 of the 110 proteins we could recompute, with a gap the size of the profile rounding. Your figure is not affected — the four per-pair means in the text (0.05, 0.07, 0.24, 0.40) cannot come from an object with one value per protein, so this is downstream of the plot. We could not find the line that causes it, and the deposited notebook does not run as written: the DTW chunk ends with `select(organism, protein, model1, model2, distance, normalized_distance)` followed by `select(organism, description, model1, model2, dtw_object)`, which asks for two columns the first `select()` just dropped and for a `description` column the frame does not have. Could you re-deposit that sheet, and the chunk that produced it?
2. **Which codon-frequency table does `kodonz::pminmax(spp = ...)` use?** We could not reproduce your `%MinMax` profiles from either candidate: frequencies counted from the natural DNA in the Fig. 3 Source Data give a mean absolute difference of 0.1525 on the 0-1 scale, and the Kazusa tables that your own repository ships a loader for give 0.0378, worst case 0.0857. Both miss the 0.02 ceiling we had set. One line naming the table, or the table itself in the Source Data, would let anyone recompute Fig. 4c from sequences rather than from your profiles.
3. **The deposited `%MinMax` values are rounded to two decimals** — `round(digits = 2)` in the chunk that writes `benchmark_minmax_profiles.tsv`, while the DTW chunk uses the unrounded profiles. All 523,068 values sit on the 0.01 grid. That is enough to shift the normalized DTW of a pair by up to 2.0e-03, and it is why our per-protein DTW values come in a little below yours rather than equal to them: 211 of 260 below, mean signed difference -2.8e-04, mean absolute 3.6e-04. The means are unaffected. Depositing the profiles unrounded would make the per-protein column exactly checkable.
4. **Which E. coli entry did the benchmark use?** The vocabulary carries three, and the paper says "Escherichia coli". `Escherichia coli general` regenerates all 52 deposited *E. coli* sequences for both checkpoints; `Escherichia coli str. K-12 substr. MG1655` regenerates none of them, though it moves the mean codon similarity for that organism by only 0.16 points. Naming the entry in the Methods would save the next person a guess.
5. **The Jaccard definition is in your code, not in the Methods.** `jaccard_similarity()` uses `base::intersect` and `base::union`, so the metric is over distinct codons; read positionwise the same data gives 0.6266 rather than 0.74. One clause in the Methods would fix the ambiguity.
6. **Pinned versions.** torch 2.7.1+cpu with transformers 4.54.0 and numpy 1.26.4 loads both checkpoints and reproduces the benchmark on CPU; the lockfile is in `env/` if it helps.
