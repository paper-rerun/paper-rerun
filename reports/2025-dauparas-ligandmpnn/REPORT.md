- Paper: J. Dauparas, G. Lee, R. Pecoraro, L. An, I. Anishchenko, S. Glasscock, D. Baker, *Nature Methods* 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41592-025-02626-1
- Code: https://github.com/dauparas/LigandMPNN @ 26ec57ac976ade5379920dbd43c7f97a91cf82de
- Data: model weights from https://files.ipd.uw.edu/pub/ligandmpnn/ (15 checkpoints, 118 MB); test structures from the RCSB for the 469 ids in `training/test_small_molecule.json` (317), `training/test_nucleotide.json` (74), `training/test_metal.json` (83); Source Data Fig. 2 from the article (41592_2025_2626_MOESM3_ESM.zip)
- Target: the six sequence-recovery numbers of Fig. 2a / the Abstract — LigandMPNN and the retrained ProteinMPNN near small molecules (63.3% / 50.5%), nucleotides (50.5% / 34.0%) and metals (77.5% / 40.6%)
- Verdict: **Partially reproduced**
- Date: 2026-10-22
- Hardware: Ryzen 9 3900X, 31 GB RAM available under WSL2 (the machine has 64 GB), RTX 2070 SUPER 8 GB, WSL2 Ubuntu

## Results

Two independent agents designed sequences with the authors' code and released weights and scored them with metric code each wrote on its own. "Exec" is the primary run; "Blind" is the recomputation done without seeing the primary numbers. Both columns are the pre-registered metric, the one fixed before anything ran: side-chain atoms within 5.0 Å of any non-protein atom. For the blind run that is the `sidechain|all` key of `verify/out/score_*.json`.

All values are percentages: per protein, the mean recovery over 10 designed sequences at the near-ligand positions; per split, the median over proteins.

Three of the six claims land inside the pre-registered tolerance in both runs (C1, C4, C6), and three miss in both (C2, C3, C5). The two runs agree with each other on every claim: the largest exec-blind difference is 1.36 pp (C5), the next 1.13 pp (C6), and the other four are under 0.4 pp. Different machines were not used, but the seeds, the input assembly for two splits and all the scoring code were independent, so the agreement says the remaining gaps are not implementation noise on our side.

The direction of all three comparisons the paper makes reproduces, in both runs. The size does not reproduce uniformly: LigandMPNN beats the retrained ProteinMPNN by 15.3 pp (exec) / 15.0 pp (blind) near small molecules against 12.8 pp published, by 22.0 / 21.9 pp near nucleotides against 16.5 pp, and by 24.3 / 26.8 pp near metals against 36.9 pp. So the gap comes out larger than published on two splits and smaller on the metal split, where the pre-registered "any non-protein atom" context pulls in sugars, buffer and cryoprotectant and dilutes the metal site (see finding 2). The qualitative claim of the paper — atomic context raises native sequence recovery near ligands, most of all near metals — holds under every reading we tried. Two of the six published levels do.

## Definitions, with the paper's own words

The paper describes no inference protocol for this benchmark: no temperature, no seed, no checkpoint file name, no chain or assembly choice, no account of how the test structures were prepared. The Methods cover training data, optimizer, architecture and the algorithm listings only. Each definition below was fixed from the paper plus the released code's defaults **before** anything ran, and is listed with the quote it came from and what the quote leaves open.

**near_residue** — a residue is scored if any of its side-chain atoms lies within 5.0 Å of any non-protein atom. Quote: "We determined the native amino-acid residue sequence recovery for positions close to the ligand (with sidechain atoms within 5.0 Å of any nonprotein atoms)." Ambiguity: the paper never says whether side-chain means heavy atoms only, whether CB counts, or what happens to glycine. Literal reading taken: every atom except N, CA, C, O and OXT; hydrogens ignored; glycine can never qualify; alanine qualifies through CB. The authors' own per-protein denominators point away from this reading and towards the Fig. 2a legend's wording — see finding 1. It stays the verdict metric because it was the pre-registered one.

**distance_cutoff** — 5.0 Å, one cutoff, on native coordinates. Quote (Fig. 2a legend): "Sequence recoveries (sec. rec.) are averaged over the residues within 5.0 Å from the context atoms." Ambiguity: the legend and the Results sentence are not the same metric. Not to be confused with `--ligand_mpnn_cutoff_for_score` (default 8.0), which feeds only the model's confidence score.

**context_atoms_ligand** — all non-protein, non-water heavy atoms of the input structure. Quote (Methods, on training data): "We parsed all residues present in the PDBs except ['HOH', 'NA', 'CL', 'K', 'BR']." Ambiguity: that sentence describes the training parse and drops Na/Cl/K/Br, while the released `parse_PDB` selects `not protein and not water`. We followed the code. The claims map also recorded a suspected bug in the released element lookup (title-case `element_list` versus `y_t.upper()`), which would have discarded every two-letter element symbol and made C5 unreachable. **That claim was wrong**, and both runs refuted it by measurement rather than by reading: see "What it took".

**designed_region** — the whole protein is redesigned; recovery is then scored only at the near-ligand positions. Quote: "We determined the native amino-acid residue sequence recovery for positions close to the ligand". Ambiguity: the paper never says distant residues were held fixed, and `--fixed_residues` defaults to empty, so a full redesign is the literal reading. Fixing distant positions to native would raise recovery; not done.

**chain_and_assembly** — small-molecule split: one protein chain plus one named ligand, `<id>_<chain><ligand>.pdb`, built from the asymmetric unit. Nucleotide and metal splits: the whole deposited file for the bare id. Quote: "For fair comparison, we retrained ProteinMPNN on the same training dataset of PDB biounits as LigandMPNN". Ambiguity: the prose never states this; it was read off the authors' Source Data Fig. 2b `names` arrays, which carry one name per scored protein. All 317 small-molecule names have the form `1a28_ASTR.pdb`; the named ligand is present in the named chain in 317/317 cases. The nucleotide and metal names are bare ids, so asymmetric unit versus biounit 1 stays open; exec fixed biounit 1, blind fixed the asymmetric unit, and we report both.

**recovery_per_sequence** — 100 × (near-ligand positions whose designed residue equals the native) / (number of near-ligand positions). Stated only implicitly; the Source Data per-protein values are consistent with it.

**aggregation** — per protein, mean over the 10 sequences; headline, median over proteins; not pooled over residues. Quote: "The median sequence recoveries (ten designed sequences per protein) near small molecules were 50.4% for Rosetta …" and "One dot represents an average sequence recovery over 10 sequences for one protein". Settled against the Source Data, where each CSV is 3 methods × n proteins and the per-method medians reproduce the published numbers.

**n_sequences_per_protein** — 10, i.e. `batch_size × number_of_batches = 10`. Quote: "(ten designed sequences per protein)". Ambiguity: none on the count, but both flags default to 1, so 10 must be asked for. No seed is reported anywhere in the paper; `run.py` reads `if args.seed`, so its default of 0 is falsy and draws a random seed. We passed explicit non-zero seeds and logged them.

**sampling_temperature** — 0.1, the released default. Quote: "During the inference, we sample from these probabilities with some temperate (T) (algorithm 13) and iteratively run algorithm 12 to populate the designed sequence (S)." (verbatim, typo included). Ambiguity: the paper gives no numeric temperature anywhere. Recovery rises as T falls, so this is a real degree of freedom. It was fixed at 0.1 before running and never varied: all 21 of our `run.py` commands carry `--temperature 0.1`, and the only `0.05` in the tree is in the authors' own README and `run_examples.sh`.

**ligandmpnn_checkpoint** — `ligandmpnn_v_32_010_25.pt`, atom context on, side-chain context off. Quote: "The baseline model was trained with 0.1 Å standard deviation noise". Ambiguity: the paper names no file. Three stated numbers pin this one (0.1 Å noise, "the 25 closest ligand atoms", K = 32), and it is also `run.py`'s default. The 0.2 Å file fits the authors' per-protein values better — see finding 3, which is a hypothesis, not a settled result.

**proteinmpnn_checkpoint** — `proteinmpnn_v_48_010.pt`. Quote: "For fair comparison, we retrained ProteinMPNN on the same training dataset of PDB biounits as LigandMPNN (the retrained model is referred to as ProteinMPNN in this Article), except none of the context atoms was provided during training." Ambiguity: it is never stated whether the `proteinmpnn_v_48_*.pt` weights shipped in this repo *are* that retrained model or the original 2022 weights. We matched the stated 0.1 Å noise rather than `run.py`'s own default of `proteinmpnn_v_48_020.pt` (0.2 Å), since the paper warns that "when comparing performance across methods, similar levels of noising must be used".

**test_set_id_lists** — the repo's own `test_small_molecule.json` (317), `test_nucleotide.json` (74), `test_metal.json` (83); 469 unique ids in all. Quote: "We held out a nonoverlapping subset of proteins that have small-molecule contexts (a total of 317), nucleotide contexts (a total of 74) and metal contexts (a total of 83)." Counts verified by reading the files. Nucleotide and metal share exactly 5 ids (1qum, 1u3e, 2nq9, 6wdz, 7kii); harmless, since the splits are scored separately.

**row_order** — row i of each Source Data file is entry i of the matching repo test list. Checked, not assumed: the `names` arrays match the three lists as sets *and* in order. This let us compare per protein, not only medians.

**split_membership** — a structure is in a split because it is on that list, not by any rule of ours. The paper gives no classification rule for "small molecule" or "transition metal".

**Tolerances are ours.** The paper reports no seed, no standard deviation and no error bar for any recovery number. We built 1.0 pp (n=317) and 2.0 pp (n=74, 83) from the paper's 0.1 pp rounding plus the ~2% effect it treats as real ("Training with 0.05 Å and 0.2 Å noise instead increased and decreased sequence recovery by about 2%"). After the runs, the blind agent bootstrapped the median over the authors' *own* published per-protein values and found the 95% half-widths to be ±1.62, ±1.75, ±2.01, ±4.03, ±4.80 and ±6.04 pp for C1–C6 (`verify/out/median_noise.json`). Our tolerances are tighter than the published statistic's own resolution in all six cases, with C3 effectively tied (2.0 against 2.01). That tension turns out not to matter here: on the pre-registered metric the two rules give the same answer, 3 of 6 inside and 3 outside, for both runs — C2, C3 and C5 fail by 2.5 to 13.2 pp, which is outside the authors' interval too. The lenient count of five-of-six passes only appears under the post-hoc readings in "Alternative readings", and those do not decide the verdict.

## What we found about the published method

**1. The Results sentence and the Fig. 2a legend describe different neighbourhoods, and the authors' denominators point to the legend.** The Results say "sidechain atoms within 5.0 Å", the Fig. 2a legend says "residues within 5.0 Å from the context atoms". We could test this from the authors' own data without touching our recoveries. Each per-protein Source Data value is a mean of ten ratios k/n, so the value's decimals pin n, and the three methods for a protein share n. Taking the n consistent across all three methods: the any-heavy-atom count matches the authors' n in 282/317 small-molecule proteins (exec) and 284/317 (blind), the side-chain count in 26/317 in both. On the nucleotide split, counting exact matches only, any-heavy-atom matches 34/74 on biounit 1 and 30/74 on the asymmetric unit (both stages agree on those two numbers), the side-chain count 3/74. The residue of disagreement is mostly exact integer multiples (1e3g 7 against 21, 1g7f 6 against 18, 1am9 33 against 66, 1fee 4 against 8), i.e. symmetric copies of the same site counted once.

What this does to the numbers runs the other way, and we state both halves. Under the any-heavy-atom reading the exec medians become 65.24 (C1) and 51.33 (C2): C2 becomes a hit and C1 a miss, the mirror image of the pre-registered reading. Per-protein agreement improves under it (exec: C1 r 0.695 → 0.811, C2 0.690 → 0.768), but that is a second reading of the same Source Data values, not independent support. So the denominators favour the legend's wording while the published LigandMPNN level fits the Results sentence's wording better. Neither reading makes both small-molecule claims hit.

**2. The metal split's context looks like metal atoms only, not every hetero atom.** The claim text says "any nonprotein atoms". That is hard to square with the authors' data: 1dwh resolves to n=3 scored residues from their decimals, while every hetero atom of the file (it is full of NAG, SO4 and GOL) gives 122 under the any-heavy-atom reading and 101 under the side-chain reading. With the context restricted to metal and ion atoms, the authors' n is matched exactly in 64/83 proteins against 15/83 for all hetero atoms and 6/83 for the pre-registered side-chain reading, and per-protein correlation roughly doubles (exec, side-chain neighbourhood: LigandMPNN 0.333 → 0.689, ProteinMPNN 0.574 → 0.783; blind, any-heavy-atom neighbourhood: LigandMPNN 0.34 → 0.78, ProteinMPNN 0.59 → 0.88). Under that reading LigandMPNN gives 81.00 (exec, biounit 1) and 80.00 (blind, asymmetric unit) against 77.5 published, instead of 64.33 / 65.69. Most of C5's apparent 13 pp collapse is the context reading, not the model.

A second caveat on this block: with the metal-only context the scored sets are tiny (the authors' own n resolve to 1, 3, 4, 12 residues; our median is 10 positions per protein against 30 under the pre-registered reading), so a per-protein recovery can only take a few discrete values and the median of 83 of them is coarse — our values land on 75.0, 80.0 or 81.0 and never on 77.5. For this split the mean is the more stable statistic, and at the pre-registered 0.1 Å checkpoint it sits above the published mean: C5 79.08 (blind) / 78.75 (exec) against 75.56, C6 42.21 (blind) / 39.66 (exec) against 41.90. The 74.76 that would land almost exactly on the published mean is the 0.2 Å checkpoint, not the 0.1 Å one; it belongs to finding 3, not to this one.

**3. The published Fig. 2a may not be the 0.1 Å LigandMPNN the Methods describe — a hypothesis, not a finding.** The Methods state 0.1 Å, and the blind agent swept the checkpoints against the authors' per-protein values. With `ligandmpnn_v_32_010_25.pt` LigandMPNN is systematically high: per-protein bias +2.25 pp (small molecules), +3.08 (nucleotides), +3.52 (metals). With `ligandmpnn_v_32_020_25.pt` (0.2 Å) the bias on the small-molecule split falls to -0.06 pp — mean 61.322 against the published 61.384, over 317 proteins — and to +1.93 and -0.80 on the others. For ProteinMPNN it runs the other way: 0.1 Å gives bias -0.47 pp, 0.2 Å gives -2.38. If that is the explanation, the published figure is LigandMPNN at 0.2 Å against ProteinMPNN at 0.1 Å, which is what the paper itself warns against, and the published gap is the conservative one.

The reason this is a hypothesis: sampling temperature was fixed at the released default 0.1 in every run and never varied, and a lower temperature produces the same sign of bias. The exec nucleotide block named temperature "the prime suspect" for exactly this +2 to +3.5 pp LigandMPNN excess and did not test it. Checkpoint and temperature are therefore not separated by anything we ran. The one thing we can say without that confound: with both models at 0.1 Å the blind run gives 63.00 against 48.00 under the pre-registered reading (15.0 pp) and 64.76 against 50.77 under the any-heavy-atom reading (14.0 pp), both wider than the published 12.8 pp.

The exec run also tested the checkpoint the definitions flagged as the first suspect for a C2 miss: `run.py`'s own default `proteinmpnn_v_48_020.pt` gives 46.11, i.e. 1.5 pp *further* from the published value, so the ProteinMPNN choice was right and does not explain C2.

## What it took

**Environment.** The authors' pins resolve as published, on Python 3.11.17 from `requirements.txt`, with one environment-only correction: `setuptools` held below 81. ProDy 2.4.1 does `import pkg_resources`, which setuptools 84 has removed, so `import prody` dies with `ModuleNotFoundError`; `setuptools==80.10.2` passes. Frozen as a 34-package lockfile (`torch==2.2.1+cu121`, `numpy==1.23.5`, `prody==2.4.1`, `biopython==1.79`, `scipy==1.12.0`) plus a rebuild script that re-clones the commit, installs from the lockfile, fetches weights and structures and runs two smoke tests. No line of the repo was changed. Both checkpoints were hash-pinned before use (`ligandmpnn_v_32_010_25.pt` sha256 161cd264…a80970, `proteinmpnn_v_48_010.pt` sha256 db866fae…35ccce); `atom_context_num` comes from the checkpoint itself, so the 25-context-atom setting was not left to a flag.

**Runtime.** Six design runs of 317 + 74 + 83 proteins × 10 sequences per model, twice over (primary and blind), on one 2070 SUPER serialised behind a single GPU lock. Roughly 20 to 35 minutes per split per model, plus long queueing: the two agents and two others shared the lock, so wall clock was several hours.

**Changes we had to make.**

*5l70, the only data hole, and a silent corruption.* 5l70 (metal split) has no legacy-PDB representation at the RCSB — 404 for both `.pdb` and `.pdb1` — and `run.py` parses with ProDy's `parsePDB`, which does not read mmCIF. We converted `5l70.cif` and `5l70-assembly1.cif` with ProDy's own `parseMMCIF` + `writePDB` (4576 atoms, 4 chains, 516 CAs). That conversion is silently corrupt: the entry's ligand has the five-character CCD code A1IJ4, the legacy `resName` field has three columns, `writePDB` writes it anyway, the name overruns into the chain and residue-number columns, and `parsePDB` then dies with `could not convert string to float` at line 4023. The conversion helper recorded `{"status": "converted"}`, so the intended 83 → 82 fallback never fired and the exec run died at entry 66 of 83. Both agents fixed it independently by shortening that one component id (exec → `LIG`, blind → `L01`) and realigning the 32 affected lines; coordinates, elements, atom names, chains and residue numbers untouched. The model reads context by element, never by residue name, so the label carries no information. Both fixed copies live beside the untouched downloads. With that, the metal split closes at 83/83. This is data preparation, not a method change, but it is the one place where published data could not be used as released.

*Ten nucleotide inputs lost their symmetry copies.* 10 of the 74 biounit-1 files hold more than one MODEL (1b01, 2zhg, 2zio, 3gna, 3lsr, 3vok, 4ato, 4e0p, 5haw, 8dwm). ProDy keeps the extra models as coordsets and `getCoords` returns only the first, so for those 10 the copies were silently not used: fewer context atoms and a smaller scored set than the file holds. That is 14% of the split whose LigandMPNN claim misses by ~4 pp. It was not re-run. It affects the exec run only — the blind run used the asymmetric unit, which has one copy by construction — and the two runs still agree on C3 to 0.18 pp, so it does not look like the cause of the C3 miss. Several of the authors' denominators resolve to exactly half our count (1am9 33 against 66, 1ec6 29 against 56), which points the other way: they may have scored one copy where we scored two.

*Nothing else in the code.* No line of the authors' code was edited in either run. The element-lookup bug the claims map predicted does not exist at this commit, and both runs refuted it by measurement rather than by reading the source: `data_utils.py:648` reassigns `element_list` to its uppercase form before `element_dict` is built on line 649, so `y_t.upper()` matches two-letter symbols normally. 1dwh keeps 349 of 349 non-protein non-water heavy atoms including its zinc, 1a0a 691 of 691, and across all 317 small-molecule inputs `parse_PDB` kept exactly the deposited non-hydrogen hetero atoms with 0 mismatches (elements seen: C 6968, O 1087, N 1056, S 129, F 104, Cl 68, P 12, Br 4, I 1). HEAD is the merge of PR #37, "parse_bug_fix", so the bug plausibly existed in an earlier release and is already fixed here. The claims map's warning that "C5 cannot be met" for that reason was simply wrong.

*Process problems, recorded for honesty.* The exec design runs were killed five times while queued on the shared GPU lock — twice in the nucleotide block, three times in the metal block — with no OOM in dmesg and 28 GB of RAM free at the time. On the blind side, one batch aborted because `run_split.sh` was edited while bash was still reading it (`unexpected EOF`), and one run was restarted deliberately to change batching from 1×10 to 5×2 (the same 10 sequences; `model_utils.sample()` has a Python loop per residue, so a batch of 5 costs almost what 1 costs, and the run fell from ~16 s to ~3.2 s per structure). Both agents made their runs restartable from per-structure stats files, so each kill cost one structure — but each restart re-seeds, so neither LigandMPNN pass is the single uninterrupted random stream the command as written would give. The paper reports no seed and the statistic is a median over 10 samples at T=0.1, so no number is affected. One trial created an empty `/trial` directory in the WSL root from an unexpanded variable, since this harness expands `$VAR` inside single-quoted `wsl … bash -lc` commands before WSL sees them; removed, and every later path was written out in full.

*A false alarm we retract.* The blind agent concluded at one point that the GPU `flock` was not serialising, because `ps | grep run.py` showed four processes. Wrong: the grep also matches the parent `flock` process, whose command line contains `run.py`. Filtering by `comm=python` shows exactly one process on the GPU. The lock works.

## Notes for the authors

Six questions, offered as a friendly issue, each one re-runnable from your own Source Data. Nothing here is a bug in the model.

1. Which neighbourhood does Fig. 2a use? The Results sentence says "sidechain atoms within 5.0 Å of any nonprotein atoms" and the legend says "residues within 5.0 Å from the context atoms", and the two tests we can run disagree. Your per-protein denominators match any-heavy-atom in 282–284 of 317 small-molecule proteins and side-chain-only in 26; but the recovery levels go the other way, since the side-chain reading puts us at 62.9–63.0 against your 63.3 while the any-heavy-atom reading puts us at 64.8–65.2 (+1.9) and ProteinMPNN at 50.8–51.3 (+0.8) against 50.5. Could you say which one the figure used, so the two sentences can be made to agree?
2. On the metal split, was the context restricted to the metal atoms? "Any nonprotein atoms" gives 1dwh 101–122 scored residues against the n=3 your decimals imply, and metal-and-ion-only context matches your n exactly in 64 of 83 proteins against 15 for all hetero atoms. That one choice is worth about 15 pp on the headline metal number for anyone reproducing it.
3. Which checkpoint files produced Fig. 2a? With `ligandmpnn_v_32_010_25.pt` (the 0.1 Å the Methods state) our per-protein bias against your small-molecule values is +2.25 pp; with `ligandmpnn_v_32_020_25.pt` it is -0.06 pp over 317 proteins, while ProteinMPNN matches best at 0.1 Å. We cannot tell a 0.2 Å LigandMPNN apart from a lower sampling temperature, because we never varied temperature — so this is a question, not a claim. Naming the two files, and the temperature, in the legend would settle both at once.
4. Could you publish the exact input files for the three splits? The 317 small-molecule inputs are recoverable from your Source Data names (`<id>_<chain><ligand>.pdb` resolves 317/317), but the nucleotide and metal names are bare ids, and neither the asymmetric unit (30/74 by your denominators) nor biounit 1 (34/74) explains them; several entries resolve to exactly half our count (1am9 33 against 66, 1ec6 29 against 56), which looks like a sub-assembly. A small text file of file names would make Fig. 2a fully reproducible.
5. `training/test_metal.json` contains 5l70, which has no legacy-PDB file at the RCSB, and ProDy's `writePDB` silently produces an unparseable file for its five-character ligand code A1IJ4 (the residue name overruns into the chain and residue-number columns, and `parsePDB` then fails at line 4023). Would an mmCIF path in `parse_PDB`, or a note in the README, be worth adding? Anyone rebuilding this split from the RCSB will hit it.

## Pipeline

**Claims map and independent check.** One agent read the article (full text as JATS XML from Europe PMC) and listed six numeric claims with a tolerance and a condition set each, plus thirteen definitions fixed from the paper before anything ran. A second agent re-verified all six paper values against the article text and all nine per-method medians against Source Data Fig. 2, recomputing the CSVs itself. It corrected C2's target from 50.4 to 50.5 (the Abstract rounds correctly, the Results sentence is the slip), replaced the "biological assembly 1, all of it" guess with the per-split file-name reading taken from the Source Data `names` arrays, added a fourteenth definition pinning row order so per-protein comparison is possible, and recorded the suspected element-lookup bug that would have made C5 unreachable — the one definition item both later runs disproved.

**Frozen environment.** Built once from the authors' `requirements.txt` on Python 3.11.17 and frozen as a 34-package lockfile with a rebuild script, weights hash-pinned, two smoke tests. One environment-only fix (`setuptools<81`, for ProDy's `pkg_resources` import). No repo line changed.

**Per-block execution.** Three blocks — small molecule, nucleotide, metal — run by separate agents with the authors' `run.py` and released weights, each GPU command behind `flock` on a shared lock, `MPLBACKEND=Agg`, no windows. Each block measured rather than assumed what the definitions left open: how many context atoms survive `parse_PDB` on a real metal case (349/349, zinc included), whether the designed and native position orders align (asserted per entry), and how much the assembly and backbone-completeness choices move the number.

**Blind recomputation.** A fourth agent rebuilt the environment from the lockfile, re-ran all six claims with its own seeds, its own input assembly and its own metric code, and never read the first agents' results. It computed the pre-registered metric alongside its alternatives, which is what makes the confirmation column in the Results table possible. It independently found the same element-lookup non-bug, independently hit and fixed 5l70, and contributed the denominator test, the metal-context test and the checkpoint sweep. Two of those were fits against the authors' published values, as said at the top of "Alternative readings", so they are evidence about definitions rather than independent agreement with the paper. It also bootstrapped the authors' own per-protein values to show our tolerances are tighter than the published statistic's resolution. One leak is declared: while diagnosing GPU contention it ran `ps aux` and saw the other stage's command lines, which revealed that stage's assembly choice and seed. Its own assembly choice was already written to disk before that, and rests on its own evidence (`<id>.pdb` is the asymmetric-unit file name at the RCSB; the biounit is `<id>.pdb1`).

**Two implementations of the same test.** The denominator test was written twice, independently. On the small-molecule split exec got 282/317 and blind 284/317 for the any-heavy-atom reading, a two-protein difference we did not chase; both got 26/317 for side-chain. On the nucleotide split the two agree exactly once the counting convention is matched: 34/74 (biounit 1) and 30/74 (asymmetric unit) exact matches in both, where exec's reported 37/74 and 33/74 also count proteins whose denominator is an exact integer multiple of ours.

**Adversarial review and revision.** A further agent read the draft of this report against the logs and the two agents' outputs, and a revision agent applied its corrections. The main ones: the blind column had been filled from the post-hoc readings instead of the pre-registered one, which inverted the per-claim verdicts and produced a false "no claim was confirmed twice" headline — under matched definitions three of six are confirmed twice; the two means quoted for the metal split came from different checkpoints; the exec metal gap is smaller than published, not larger; the lenient bootstrap count applied only to the blind post-hoc values; "as the protocol requires" overstated what the pipeline rules say about self-built tolerances; note 1 to the authors had the direction of the 1.9 pp reversed; the checkpoint finding was asserted without testing temperature; the 10 multi-MODEL nucleotide files were missing; the interruption counts and the RAM figure were wrong; and the C6 "hit" is a median coincidence rather than per-protein agreement — under the pre-registered reading exec per-protein r is 0.574 with mean |diff| 15.4 pp, so it must not be read as confirmation of the metal numbers.

## How this report was made

This reproduction was run by AI agents (Claude, by Anthropic) on a home computer and reviewed by a human before publication. No number in this report was estimated or typed by hand: each one comes from code that ran on this machine, and the commands, logs and outputs are in this folder.

1. One agent read the paper and listed the numeric claims to check. Every definition the computation needs (cutoffs, test sets, how results are averaged) was fixed from the paper text before anything ran. A second agent checked that list against the paper.
2. One agent built the software environment once and froze it, starting from the versions the authors used.
3. Separate agents computed each group of claims with the authors' code and released weights, on one RTX 2070 SUPER (8 GB) and a Ryzen 9 3900X.
4. A different agent rebuilt the environment from the frozen lockfile and recomputed every claim without seeing the first results, using scoring code it wrote itself.
5. One agent wrote this report, another one tried to find errors in it, and a third applied the corrections.
6. A human read the report and approved publication.

We do this because most published code is never re-run by anyone else, and AI agents make that check cheap. The method, prompts and scripts are public at https://github.com/paper-rerun/paper-rerun/blob/main/METHOD.md, so anyone can run the same checks on papers in their own field.
